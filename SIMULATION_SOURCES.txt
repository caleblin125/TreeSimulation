Simulation constants research notes
===================================

Units and time
--------------
The simulation now keeps mechanical quantities in SI-like units: meters, kilograms,
newtons, joules, pascals, and seconds. Plant physiology is accelerated with
BiologicalSecondsPerSimulationSecond = 86400, so one simulation second is treated
as one biological day for photosynthesis, respiration, and growth.

Wood density
------------
Value used: BranchWoodDensityKgPerM3 = 506 kg/m^3.

Source: Zhao et al., "Wood Density and Fiber Dimensions of Root, Stem, and Branch
Wood of Populus ussuriensis Kom. Trees", BioResources.
https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/resources/wood-density-and-fiber-dimensions-of-root-stem-and-branch-wood-of-populus-ussuriensis-kom-trees/

Reasoning: The article reports branch wood as the lowest-density position for
Populus ussuriensis, with branch density about 0.506 g/cm^3. Converted to SI,
that is 506 kg/m^3.

Bending strength / branch failure
---------------------------------
Value used: BranchBendingStrengthPa = 66 MPa, with StructuralSafetyFactor = 0.35.

Sources:
Forest Products Laboratory, "Mechanical Properties of Wood", Wood Handbook,
Chapter 5.
https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/fplgtr/fplgtr282/chapter_05_fpl_gtr282.pdf

Stod et al., "Modulus of Elasticity and Bending Strength of Scots Pine
(Pinus sylvestris L.) Wood from Commercial Thinnings", Forests 2024.
https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/15/3/567

Reasoning: FPL describes modulus of rupture as a bending-strength criterion
proportional to maximum moment. Scots pine thinning wood reports bending strength
around 66 MPa for first-thinning material and about 80 MPa for older material.
The sim uses 66 MPa and an explicit safety factor because living branches have
knots, taper, dynamic loading, growth defects, and attachment weaknesses that are
not represented by clear-wood lab tests.

For a circular branch cross-section, max bending moment is approximated as:
M = sigma * Z, where Z = pi * r^3 / 4.

Photosynthesis
--------------
Value used: MaxLeafAssimilationMolCo2PerM2Second = 12 umol CO2/m^2/s.

Sources:
Soil Lab Modules, "Photosynthesis".
https://labmodules.soilweb.ca/photosynthesis/

LibreTexts Biology, "Photosynthesis - Pathway of Carbon Fixation".
https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_and_General_Biology/Biology_%28Kimball%29/04%3A_Cell_Metabolism/4.07%3A_Photosynthesis_-_Pathway_of_Carbon_Fixation

CliffsNotes, "Energetics of Photosynthesis".
https://www.cliffsnotes.com/study-guides/biology/biochemistry-ii/photosynthesis/energetics-of-photosynthesis

Reasoning: Soil Lab defines net assimilation An in umol CO2 m^-2 s^-1 and leaf
surface area S in m^2. The sim uses a typical order-of-magnitude leaf
assimilation value of 12 umol CO2/m^2/s. LibreTexts gives the glucose
photosynthesis stoichiometry of 6 CO2 per glucose. CliffsNotes gives about
2870 kJ needed to synthesize 1 mol glucose, so the chemical energy stored per
mol CO2 fixed is 2.87e6 / 6 = 478333 J/mol CO2.

The current model has no leaves, so leaf area is estimated as:
leafArea = branchLateralSurfaceArea * LeafAreaPerBranchSurfaceArea.
This is a deliberate placeholder until explicit leaves are modeled.

Woody respiration / maintenance
-------------------------------
Value used: WoodyRespirationMolCo2PerM2Second = 0.8 umol CO2/m^2/s.

Source: Asao, Bedoya-Arrieta, and Ryan, "Variation in foliar respiration and
wood CO2 efflux rates among species and canopy layers in a wet tropical forest",
US Forest Service / Tree Physiology.
https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/48611

Reasoning: The summary reports wood CO2 efflux around 0.6-0.9 umol/m^2/s for
understory species and 1.0-1.6 umol/m^2/s for overstory trees. The sim uses the
low end, 0.8 umol/m^2/s, because simulated trees are young and small. Respired
CO2 is converted back to chemical energy cost with the same glucose-energy
conversion used for photosynthesis.

Energy model
------------
Value used: WoodConstructionEnergyJPerKg = 17 MJ/kg.

Reasoning: This is a broad dry-biomass heat-content approximation used here as a
construction energy scale for new wood. It is intentionally rough: real growth
cost depends on carbon use efficiency, tissue chemistry, water content, nutrient
status, and growth respiration.

Seed reserve energy
-------------------
Values used:
AverageTreeSeedDryMassKg = 0.000488 kg.
SeedReserveEnergyDensityJPerKg = 20 MJ/kg.
InitialEnergyJ = about 9760 J.

Sources:
Global Patterns in the Evolutionary Relations Between Seed Mass and Plant
Functional Traits, Scientific Reports / PMC.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12158795/

FAO, "Food energy - methods of analysis and conversion factors".
https://www.fao.org/4/y5022e/y5022e04.htm

JSTOR abstract, "Energy Values of Tree-Seeds Eaten by Small Mammals".
https://www.jstor.org/stable/3543838

Reasoning: The global tree-seed dataset reports most tree species between 0.1
and 10,000 mg seed mass, with an average around 488 mg. FAO Atwater factors put
carbohydrate/protein around 17 kJ/g and fat around 37 kJ/g. Tree-seed caloric
values reported for whole seeds are about 4.4-6.8 kcal/g, or roughly
18-28 kJ/g. The sim uses 20 kJ/g as a conservative mixed-reserve value:
0.488 g * 20 kJ/g = 9.76 kJ = 9760 J.

Root uptake
-----------
Value used: RootSupportedLeafAreaPerRootArea = 20.

Reasoning: This is a modeling placeholder, not a researched biological
constant. Each seedling now starts with a downward root limb. Root lateral
surface area is treated as water/nutrient absorbing area, and photosynthesis is
scaled by:

min(1, rootSurfaceArea * RootSupportedLeafAreaPerRootArea / leafArea)

This makes roots gate photosynthesis, but it does not yet model root hairs,
mycorrhizae, soil water potential, nitrogen/phosphorus availability, or hydraulic
conductance.

Genetic variation
-----------------
Each organism now carries a Genome struct containing heritable growth,
structure, photosynthesis, root uptake, and reproduction parameters. Seeds carry
mutated copies of the parent genome. Mutation is currently multiplicative
Gaussian noise with clamped bounds; there is no sexual recombination yet.

Major known simplifications
---------------------------
1. There are no explicit leaves. Photosynthesis is estimated from branch surface
   area times an artificial leaf-area multiplier.
2. Roots are simple downward limbs. There is no branching root architecture,
   root hairs, soil, water storage, nutrient pools, hydraulic limits, or drought.
3. Branch strength ignores taper, knots, reaction wood, branch collars, and wind.
4. The torque model uses static gravity only; no dynamic loads or self-collision.
5. Energy is a single organism-wide pool, not local carbohydrate transport.
6. Seeds germinate anywhere on the ground; there is no soil, shade competition,
   dormancy, predation, or establishment mortality.
7. Genetics are asexual only. There is no recombination, ploidy, developmental
   gene regulation, or speciation tracking yet.
