Simulation constants research notes
===================================

Units and time
--------------
The simulation now keeps mechanical quantities in SI-like units: meters, kilograms,
newtons, joules, pascals, and seconds. The world simulation advances in years,
with the default visual step set to one month. Photosynthesis and respiration use
SecondsPerYear and ActivePhotosynthesisFractionOfYear to convert year-sized steps
back to active physiological seconds.

Wood density
------------
Value used: BranchWoodDensityKgPerM3 = 506 kg/m^3.

Source: Zhao et al., "Wood Density and Fiber Dimensions of Root, Stem, and Branch
Wood of Populus ussuriensis Kom. Trees", BioResources.
https://bioresources.cnr.ncsu.edu/resources/wood-density-and-fiber-dimensions-of-root-stem-and-branch-wood-of-populus-ussuriensis-kom-trees/

Reasoning: The article reports branch wood as the lowest-density position for
Populus ussuriensis, with branch density about 0.506 g/cm^3. Converted to SI,
that is 506 kg/m^3.

Bending strength / branch failure
---------------------------------
Value used: BranchBendingStrengthPa = 66 MPa, with StructuralSafetyFactor = 0.35.

Sources:
Forest Products Laboratory, "Mechanical Properties of Wood", Wood Handbook,
Chapter 5.
https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/fplgtr/fplgtr282/chapter_05_fpl_gtr282.pdf

Stod et al., "Modulus of Elasticity and Bending Strength of Scots Pine
(Pinus sylvestris L.) Wood from Commercial Thinnings", Forests 2024.
https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/15/3/567

Reasoning: FPL describes modulus of rupture as a bending-strength criterion
proportional to maximum moment. Scots pine thinning wood reports bending strength
around 66 MPa for first-thinning material and about 80 MPa for older material.
The sim uses 66 MPa and an explicit safety factor because living branches have
knots, taper, dynamic loading, growth defects, and attachment weaknesses that are
not represented by clear-wood lab tests.

For a circular branch cross-section, max bending moment is approximated as:
M = sigma * Z, where Z = pi * r^3 / 4.

Photosynthesis
--------------
Value used: MaxLeafAssimilationMolCo2PerM2Second = 12 umol CO2/m^2/s.

Sources:
Soil Lab Modules, "Photosynthesis".
https://labmodules.soilweb.ca/photosynthesis/

LibreTexts Biology, "Photosynthesis - Pathway of Carbon Fixation".
https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_and_General_Biology/Biology_%28Kimball%29/04%3A_Cell_Metabolism/4.07%3A_Photosynthesis_-_Pathway_of_Carbon_Fixation

CliffsNotes, "Energetics of Photosynthesis".
https://www.cliffsnotes.com/study-guides/biology/biochemistry-ii/photosynthesis/energetics-of-photosynthesis

Reasoning: Soil Lab defines net assimilation An in umol CO2 m^-2 s^-1 and leaf
surface area S in m^2. The sim uses a typical order-of-magnitude leaf
assimilation value of 12 umol CO2/m^2/s. LibreTexts gives the glucose
photosynthesis stoichiometry of 6 CO2 per glucose. CliffsNotes gives about
2870 kJ needed to synthesize 1 mol glucose, so the chemical energy stored per
mol CO2 fixed is 2.87e6 / 6 = 478333 J/mol CO2.

The current model has no leaves, so leaf area is estimated as:
leafArea = branchLateralSurfaceArea * LeafAreaPerBranchSurfaceArea.
This is a deliberate placeholder until explicit leaves are modeled.

Woody respiration / maintenance
-------------------------------
Value used: WoodyMaintenanceRespirationMolCo2PerM3Second = 6.5 umol
CO2/m^3/s.

Sources:
Ryan, Linder, Vose, and Hubbard, "Dark respiration of pines", Ecological
Bulletins / Oregon State PDF mirror.
https://people.forestry.oregonstate.edu/richard-waring/sites/richard-waring/files/publications/69.pdf

Ryan et al., "Woody tissue maintenance respiration of four conifers in
contrasting climates", Oecologia / PubMed.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28306783/

Reasoning: The conifer study reports stem maintenance respiration linearly
related to sapwood volume, with sapwood-volume rates around 4.8-8.3 umol
CO2/m^3/s at 10 C and Q10 around 1.7. It also estimates aboveground woody
maintenance as about 5-13% of net daytime carbon assimilation annually. The sim
uses the middle of the reported sapwood-volume range, 6.5 umol CO2/m^3/s, and
applies it to the simplified whole-cylinder limb volume. This is more fitting
than the older surface-efflux placeholder because current limbs are wood
cylinders without bark/sapwood layering.

Energy reserve decay
--------------------
Value used: EnergyReserveTurnoverFractionPerYear = 0.08.

Sources:
Dietze et al., "Nonstructural Carbon in Woody Plants", Annual Review of Plant
Biology.
https://people.bu.edu/dietze/manuscripts/annurev-arplant-050213-040054.pdf

Richardson et al., "Seasonal dynamics and age of stemwood nonstructural
carbohydrates in temperate forest trees", New Phytologist / USDA Forest Service.
https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/context/usdafsfacpub/article/1229/viewcontent/Richardson_NP_2012_Seasonal_dynamics_and_age_of_stemwood.pdf

Martinez-Vilalta et al., "Dynamics of non-structural carbohydrates in terrestrial
plants", Functional Ecology / PMC.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6288742/

Reasoning: Nonstructural carbohydrates support growth, metabolism, defense,
recovery, and seasonal stress. The literature separates faster-turnover
accumulated carbon from slower stored pools, and stemwood NSC is observed to
decline during winter and spring when it supports maintenance and growth. The
sim keeps one organism-wide energy store, so it uses a conservative 8% annual
turnover/decay on positive stored energy to prevent founders from banking energy
forever without adding a full seasonal carbohydrate-pool model.

Energy model
------------
Value used: WoodConstructionEnergyJPerKg = 17 MJ/kg.

Reasoning: This is a broad dry-biomass heat-content approximation used here as a
construction energy scale for new wood. It is intentionally rough: real growth
cost depends on carbon use efficiency, tissue chemistry, water content, nutrient
status, and growth respiration.

Seed reserve energy
-------------------
Values used:
AverageTreeSeedDryMassKg = 0.000488 kg.
SeedReserveEnergyDensityJPerKg = 24 MJ/kg.
InitialEnergyJ = about 11712 J.
SeedProvisioningCostMultiplier = 4.

Sources:
Global Patterns in the Evolutionary Relations Between Seed Mass and Plant
Functional Traits, Scientific Reports / PMC.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12158795/

FAO, "Food energy - methods of analysis and conversion factors".
https://www.fao.org/4/y5022e/y5022e04.htm

Kager and Fietz, "Energy or information? The role of seed availability for
reproductive decisions in edible dormice", Journal of Comparative Physiology B.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00360-009-0425-6

JSTOR abstract, "Energy Values of Tree-Seeds Eaten by Small Mammals".
https://www.jstor.org/stable/3543838

Smith et al., "Individual-level variation in reproductive effort in chestnut oak
and black oak", Forest Ecology and Management 508 (2022).
https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/69489.pdf

Reasoning: The global tree-seed dataset reports most tree species between 0.1
and 10,000 mg seed mass, with an average around 488 mg. FAO Atwater factors put
carbohydrate/protein around 17 kJ/g and fat around 37 kJ/g. Tree-seed caloric
values reported for whole seeds are about 4.4-6.8 kcal/g, or roughly
18-28 kJ/g. Kager and Fietz report beech seeds around 24.1 kJ/g. The sim
uses 24 kJ/g:

0.488 g * 24 kJ/g = 11.712 kJ = 11712 J.

The parent pays more than the embryo reserve. SeedEnergyCostJ is now the seed
reserve multiplied by SeedProvisioningCostMultiplier = 4, representing seed coat,
fruit/flower/support tissue, failed ovules, dispersal structures, and respiratory
costs of reproduction. Reproduction is also gated by age, height, minimum reserve
energy, and a maximum annual reproductive allocation fraction. This follows the
ecological point that seed production depends on tree size and resources; Smith
et al. report larger DBH as a consistent predictor of acorn production and note
trade-offs between growth and reproduction.

Propagule model
---------------
The simulation uses "propagule" as the general reproductive dispersal unit.
A seed is a high-reserve propagule with protective/provisioning tissues. A spore
can be represented by the same model with much lower reserve energy, lower parent
cost, many more propagules per event, wider dispersal, and lower establishment
probability.

Current default is seed-like:
PropaguleEstablishmentProbability = 0.35.
SeedMinDispersalRadiusM = 3 m.
SeedMaxDispersalRadiusM = 40 m.

The default seed dispersal radius is intentionally broader than the first
prototype value to reduce parent/offspring clumping and create more spatial
competition. It should eventually become a heritable dispersal trait with
different modes for gravity, wind, water, animal dispersal, and spore-like
propagules.

When a propagule reaches its establishment time, it is removed from the pending
pool. It either becomes a new organism or fails to establish. This represents
non-viable seed/spore losses, unsuitable microsites, drying, predation, disease,
and other early establishment failures.

Root uptake
-----------
Value used: RootSupportedLeafAreaPerRootArea = 20.

Reasoning: This is a modeling placeholder, not a researched biological
constant. Each seedling now starts with a downward root limb. Root lateral
surface area is treated as water/nutrient absorbing area, and photosynthesis is
scaled by:

min(1, rootSurfaceArea * RootSupportedLeafAreaPerRootArea / leafArea)

This makes roots gate photosynthesis, but it does not yet model root hairs,
mycorrhizae, soil water potential, nitrogen/phosphorus availability, or hydraulic
conductance.

Soil depletion and competition
------------------------------
Values used:
SoilCellSizeM = 2 m.
SoilRecoveryPerYear = 0.12.
SoilDepletionPerRootAreaYear = 0.35.
SoilCompetitionRadiusM = 2.5 m.
SoilCompetitionPressureScale = 3.0.

Reasoning: This is a coarse competition model rather than a researched soil
chemistry model. Each occupied soil cell stores a normalized resource level from
0 to 1. Root surface area depletes the local cell over time, while depleted
cells slowly recover. Neighboring roots add instantaneous competition pressure
inside a small radius, reducing the soil factor passed into root-supported
photosynthesis and hydraulic support. Propagule establishment is also scaled by
the local soil resource, so seeds landing in exhausted patches are less likely
to become plants. This should discourage persistent clumping without yet
simulating separate water, nitrogen, phosphorus, microbial communities, or
diffusion between cells.

Sunlight competition / occlusion
--------------------------------
Value used: ExternalLimbShadeTransmission = 0.35.

Reasoning: The world now gives each organism nearby external limb candidates
from a quadtree broad phase. The organism's existing ray-sampled limb sunlight
model checks those candidates in addition to self-occlusion. External limb
occlusion transmits 35% of the sample's light instead of blocking all of it,
roughly representing partial canopy transmission before explicit leaves and
leaf density are modeled.

Xylem hydraulic and structural tradeoff
---------------------------------------
Values used:
XylemReferenceConduitDiameterM = 40 micrometers.
XylemReferenceConductiveAreaFraction = 0.18.
XylemPathResistancePerMeter = 0.08.
XylemBaseEmbolismLossFraction = 0.04.
XylemConductiveAreaStrengthPenalty = 0.70.

Sources:
Gleason et al., "Weak tradeoff between xylem safety and xylem-specific hydraulic
efficiency across the world's woody plant species", New Phytologist.
https://sperry.biology.utah.edu/publications/Gleason2016.pdf

Zheng and Martinez-Cabrera et al., "Wood anatomical correlates with theoretical
conductivity and wood density across China: evolutionary evidence of the
functional differentiation of axial and radial parenchyma", Annals of Botany /
PMC.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3747806/

Pratt et al., "Wood allocation trade-offs between fiber wall, fiber lumen, and
axial parenchyma drive drought acclimation in a conifer", Plant, Cell &
Environment / PMC.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7155043/

Hacke, Sperry, Pockman, Davis, and McCulloh, "Trends in wood density and
structure are linked to prevention of xylem implosion by negative pressure",
Oecologia.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s004420000589

Reasoning: Water transport through an ideal conduit scales very strongly with
diameter, so the sim uses conduitDiameter^4 as a first-order Hagen-Poiseuille
proxy for flow capacity. That creates an efficiency benefit for wider xylem.
The sim also applies a diameter-squared embolism loss term that increases with
tree height/path tension, because wider conduits and larger connected vessels
are generally more vulnerable to gas blockage, while acknowledging that pit
structure, conduit position, vessel density, and network topology complicate the
global safety-efficiency relationship. Conductive area fraction increases flow
but reduces effective bending strength, representing cross-section allocated to
conduits rather than supportive fiber wall. This is intentionally a trait-level
evolutionary pressure, not a full water-potential or soil-moisture model.

Genetic variation
-----------------
Each organism now carries a Genome struct containing heritable growth,
structure, photosynthesis, root uptake, and reproduction parameters. Seeds carry
mutated copies of the parent genome. Mutation is currently multiplicative
Gaussian noise with clamped bounds; there is no sexual recombination yet.

Major known simplifications
---------------------------
1. There are no explicit leaves. Photosynthesis is estimated from branch surface
   area times an artificial leaf-area multiplier.
2. Soil is a normalized grid resource with local depletion and recovery, not
   explicit water, nitrogen, phosphorus, diffusion, pH, microbes, or drought.
   Roots are still simple downward limbs without root hairs or mycorrhizae.
3. Branch strength ignores taper, knots, reaction wood, branch collars, and wind.
4. The torque model uses static gravity only; no dynamic loads or self-collision.
5. Energy is a single organism-wide pool, not local carbohydrate transport.
6. Propagules establish anywhere on the ground; there is no soil, shade
   competition, dormancy bank, predation, or microsite-specific establishment
   model yet.
7. Genetics are asexual only. There is no recombination, ploidy, developmental
   gene regulation, or speciation tracking yet.
8. Reproduction is still simplified. There is no flowering, pollination,
   self-incompatibility, masting synchrony, fruit abortion, or seed predation.
